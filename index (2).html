Your first research session

01 / Identify the instrument

A stock is company ownership. An ETF holds or tracks a basket or exposure. An index is a reference measure. A bond is debt; a bond ETF has its own fluctuating share price. Spot crypto is distinct from a crypto option. QQQ, the Nasdaq Composite and Nasdaq-100 are different instruments.

02 / Check the evidence clock

Look for the source, observation time, market session and stale-data flags. A candle summarizes open, high, low and close over an interval; its current unfinished shape can change. US chart widgets are display-only and do not feed the engines.

03 / Ask what could disprove it

Compare Tokyo’s activity with Palermo’s price structure and Stockholm’s quoted liquidity. Read Denver’s objections. In Media Desk, check publication time, primary sources and whether a headline is already reflected in prices. A regional link is a research question, not a directional forecast.

04 / Record before the outcome

Write down the idea, available evidence, rejection reasons and maximum possible loss. Compare outcomes against a benchmark after costs. Keep losing and rejected observations too. The paper ledger is not yet an operating, audited execution system.

Why the pieces belong together

Charts describe price behavior; the engines organize measurements; Media Desk adds context; the journal preserves what was known; the Evidence view asks whether the rules survive testing. The Scenario lab explores assumptions. The Robinhood path keeps account access and order permission separate from research. This structure can help you ask better questions and spot contradictions before committing money.

Read the score correctly

The proposed composite combines Tokyo (25%), Palermo (25%), inverted Denver risk (20%), Stockholm (15%) and risk control (15%). It is a research ranking, not a probability of profit. A score of 80 does not mean an 80% win rate. Current spot metrics do not implement a calibrated options scoring system. A failed hard gate takes priority over any score.

Read the evidence correctly

The rollout requires 300 historical observations, three chronological unseen test windows, 100 timestamped paper trades and 50 reconciled shadow decisions. Counts alone do not pass the system: costs, drawdown, sample adequacy, leakage and concentration must also be assessed. A green feed check only describes data health.

Calculations and boundaries

Tokyo compares the latest closed 1-minute volume to the previous 20-bar mean. Its taker-buy share uses actual taker-buy base volume; this is not institutional options flow. Palermo uses SMA5/SMA20, a simple mean of 14 true ranges (not Wilder-smoothed ATR), and 20-bar VWAP = quote volume / base volume. Denver requires 21 contiguous closed bars, latest bar age ≤90 seconds and quote age ≤10 seconds, with a connected feed. Stockholm reports the spot bid/ask spread and quoted top-level notional; it does not infer deep liquidity or executable fills.

Descriptive research flags: relative volume ≥2×; spot spread >10 bps; quoted size below 50 USDT per side. These are uncalibrated versioned defaults, not statistically proven trade thresholds. Professor is deterministic reporting, not an LLM. Missing options chains, Greeks, IV, events, fees, eligibility, and validation always block options decisions. A successful data check is not a passed trust gate.

One public REST request per pair seeds up to 120 bars when you connect; only completed bars are retained. Closed candles then arrive over the public stream. Failed requests are visible and not retried automatically. Session buffers retain 180 bars/pair and 300 selected-pair snapshots; they are not immutable or durable, and are not paper trades. Reopening the page clears them. Export before closing.

How to read a catalyst

1. Confirm the underlying fact with a primary release.

2. Compare with expectations and what is already priced in.

3. Identify affected companies and the transmission mechanism.

4. Check market reaction, options IV and executable liquidity.

No percentage price targets or probabilities are inferred from headline tone. Syndicated stories are not independent confirmation. Reddit posts are unverified community opinions.

Data and trust boundaries

Only titles, source links, publication times and supplied metadata are processed. No paywalls are bypassed. Loaded text is untrusted data, never agent instructions. Exact URL duplicates are removed; independent corroboration and story-level deduplication are not implemented.

For historical tests, publication time is insufficient: first-seen time, revisions and the information available at each decision must be archived. Imports here do not create point-in-time audit evidence.

A future server-side connector must manage publisher permissions, secrets, refresh cadence, caching and feed errors. Chat plugin installation alone does not connect this page. All-outlet coverage is unverified.

Live spot analytics are separate from the toy Scenario lab. No options contracts or paper fills are generated. Options involve substantial risk, including loss of the entire premium.
